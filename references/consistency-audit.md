# Abstract Consistency Audit Protocol

Use this protocol to verify every material statement in an abstract against inspected manuscript content.

## Evidence Sources

Trace statements to the most authoritative manuscript location:

- study design and population: Methods;
- sample size and exclusions: Methods, participant flow, or approved dataset record;
- quantitative and qualitative findings: Results, verified tables, figures, or supplementary results;
- interpretation: Discussion or Conclusion, anchored to supporting Results;
- review corpus and screening: Methods, search protocol, or evidence workbook;
- contribution and scope: manuscript objectives, Methods, Results, and bounded Conclusion together.

An existing abstract is a comparison target, not an independent evidence source.

## Statement-Level Audit Record

For each material abstract statement, record:

### Abstract statement

Quote or precisely identify the statement being checked.

### Statement type

Classify it as Background, Objective, Method, Result, Insight, Conclusion, Implication, or Keyword.

### Manuscript evidence location

Give a verified section, subsection, table, figure, supplementary item, or paragraph anchor. Do not invent a page or locator. If exact location is unavailable, write `Precise locator not verified` and identify what material was inspected.

### Supporting manuscript evidence

Summarize the actual method, observation, result, or bounded interpretation that supports the abstract statement.

### Consistency status

Choose one:

- **Directly supported:** the manuscript states or demonstrates the same claim at the same scope.
- **Faithful compression:** the abstract accurately condenses multiple manuscript statements without increasing claim strength.
- **Qualified inference:** the interpretation is reasonable and explicitly bounded, but is not itself a direct result.
- **Overstated:** the manuscript supports a narrower, more conditional, or non-causal statement.
- **Unsupported:** inspected materials do not support the statement.
- **Not verifiable from supplied materials:** the necessary manuscript content was unavailable or incomplete.

### Required action

Choose Retain, Narrow, Split, Remove, Replace with verified value, Add qualification, or Ask author to confirm. Give the smallest wording-level correction that resolves the issue.

### Boundary of interpretation

State what the evidence does not establish, including causal, population, scale, setting, temporal, validation, or generalizability boundaries.

## Result and Discussion Boundary

A Discussion passage may interpret a result but cannot create one. For any statement phrased as a finding:

1. locate the supporting Result, table, or figure;
2. verify direction, comparator, units, denominator, uncertainty, and statistical wording;
3. use Discussion only to determine the manuscript's bounded interpretation;
4. remove or relabel the statement if no result evidence exists.

Do not convert hypotheses, possible mechanisms, future work, recommendations, or literature comparisons into study findings.

## Insight Audit

For each insight included in the abstract, record:

- the research objective it answers;
- the supporting result or result combination;
- the corresponding bounded interpretation in Discussion or Conclusion, when available;
- any qualification whose omission would change meaning;
- its priority relative to other candidate insights.

The abstract may contain no more than three insights. Prefer one or two when sufficient. Remove the lowest-priority insight when it adds detail without changing the paper's main understanding.

## Finding Logic and Final Value

Check the abstract as a connected argument in addition to checking individual statements:

- Selected findings directly answer the primary question and substantiate the central contribution.
- Results proceed from the main finding to decisive support and any condition needed to understand it.
- Method names, indicators, and numbers have a clear explanatory role. A list of analyses or values does not substitute for a finding.
- With numbers and abbreviations temporarily removed, Results sentences still communicate specific observations. The final text retains decisive quantitative evidence and its interpretation context.
- Negative findings and conditions whose omission would change the conclusion remain visible.
- The final sentence identifies the paper's specific scientific, methodological, or practical value, connects to the opening problem, and follows from inspected results. Audit it as a Conclusion or Implication using the statement-level record. Generic importance claims and unsupported promised benefits require revision. For an incomplete study, check that the value is explicitly expected.

## Protected Information

Preserve qualifications needed to prevent:

- correlation-to-causation drift;
- laboratory-to-field generalization;
- component-to-system extrapolation;
- one-dataset-to-general-superiority claims;
- proxy-to-endpoint substitution;
- design or optimization claims being presented as demonstrated outcome improvement;
- search-bounded novelty language being converted into an absolute priority claim.

Word pressure is not a reason to delete a necessary hedge. Shorten background, method detail, or secondary findings first.

## Audit Summary

End the author-facing audit with:

- number of material statements checked;
- number in each consistency status;
- number of insights retained;
- unresolved author confirmations;
- whether the clean abstract is safe to use as written, safe after listed corrections, or not yet verifiable.

For audit-only requests, preserve supported text and propose targeted changes. Do not rewrite the entire abstract merely to impose a preferred voice.
